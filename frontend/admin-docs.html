SECTION 01 FOUNDATIONAL SCIENCE GEOPHYSICS

Theory of Seismology & Earthquake Mechanics

Geophysical mechanics of tectonic rupture, seismic wave propagation dynamics, logarithmic magnitude scales, and energy dissipation laws.

An earthquake is the transient mechanical vibration of the Earth's crust caused by the sudden release of accumulated elastic strain energy along geological fractures known as faults. The physics of seismology governs how strain builds up over decadal to millennial cycles and releases in fractions of a second.

1.1 Plate Tectonics & Fault Slip Mechanics

The Earth's rigid outer lithosphere is partitioned into major and minor tectonic plates floating atop the ductile, viscoelastic asthenosphere. Continuous relative plate velocities (typically 10 to 100 mm/year) generate severe boundary deformation driven by mantle convection, slab pull, and ridge push forces.

MECHANICS
Reid's Elastic Rebound Theory
Formulated after the 1906 San Francisco earthquake: tectonic forces continuously distort crustal blocks until frictional shear resistance along locked asperities is exceeded. The rocks snap back elastically to an unstrained configuration, radiating accumulated strain energy as seismic waves.
FRICTION
Stick-Slip Fault Dynamics
Fault interfaces follow rate-and-state friction. During the inter-seismic period, the fault remains locked ("stuck"). When ambient shear stress overcomes static friction (τs), dynamic slip nucleates and accelerates at rupture velocities of ≈ 2.5 – 3.5 km/s.
GEOMETRY
Hypocenter vs. Epicenter
The hypocenter (focal point) is the exact 3D subterranean coordinate (x, y, z) where fault dislocation initiates. The epicenter is the geographical projection on the surface directly above the hypocenter (x, y, 0).
Structural Fault Classifications
Normal Faults: Extensional tectonic regimes where the hanging wall drops relative to the footwall (e.g. continental rifts, East African Rift, Basin & Range).
Reverse / Thrust Faults: Compressional regimes where the hanging wall is driven upward over the footwall. Megathrust faults along oceanic subduction zones generate the planet's largest earthquakes (Mw > 8.5).
Strike-Slip Faults: Lateral shear regimes with near-vertical fault planes where blocks slip horizontally past each other (dextral / right-lateral vs. sinistral / left-lateral).

1.2 Seismic Wave Propagation Physics

Rupture along a fault plane radiates kinetic energy as elastic strain waves that propagate both through the planetary interior (body waves) and along the crustal interface (surface waves).

Wave Type Classification Particle Motion Velocity Range Propagation Medium Engineering Impact
P-Wave (Primary) Body (Compressional) Longitudinal; alternating compression and rarefaction parallel to ray path 5.5 – 8.0 km/s Solids, Liquids, Gases First to arrive; produces initial acoustic rumble and vertical jolt; low structural damage.
S-Wave (Secondary) Body (Shear) Transverse; shear motion perpendicular to propagation ray path 3.0 – 4.5 km/s Solids ONLY (μ = 0 in fluids) Second arrival; causes strong lateral shaking; triggers structural shearing and soil liquefaction.
Rayleigh Wave Surface Boundary Retrograde elliptical roll in vertical plane along propagation path ~ 0.9 × v_s Free Surface Boundary Long period; high amplitude; creates rolling ground sensation and large-scale ground displacement.
Love Wave Surface Boundary Horizontally polarized transverse shear in crustal low-velocity waveguide ~ 3.0 – 4.2 km/s Layered Crustal Solids Severe horizontal ground shear; destructive to bridges, foundations, and high-rise structures.

1.3 Magnitude Scales & Energy Release Laws

Earthquake magnitude is a logarithmic quantification of radiated seismic energy and physical rupture dimensions. CEMA prioritizes non-saturating scales to maintain true dynamic range.

Moment Magnitude Scale (Mw)
Mw = ⅔ log₁₀(M₀) - 6.07
Where M0 = μ · A · D̄ is scalar seismic moment in Newton-meters (N·m), μ is crustal shear modulus (≈ 30 GPa), A is rupture area (m²), and D̄ is average fault displacement (m). Unlike the Richter scale, Mw never saturates on great earthquakes.
Gutenberg-Richter Energy Equation
log₁₀(E) = 4.8 + 1.5 · M
Relates magnitude M to radiated seismic energy E in Joules (J). Because the scaling exponent is 1.5, each +1.0 magnitude step multiplies released seismic energy by 101.5 ≈ 31.62×. A +2.0 increase represents exactly 1,000× more energy.

1.4 Statistical Seismology: Gutenberg-Richter Recurrence

Across any active tectonic boundary, the frequency of occurrence of earthquakes follows an inverse power-law distribution known as the Gutenberg-Richter relation:

Frequency-Magnitude Recurrence Relation
log₁₀ N(≥ M) = a - b · M
Where N(≥ M) is the cumulative count of earthquakes of magnitude ≥ M, a denotes regional seismicity productivity, and b is the slope parameter (typically close to 1.0).

Physical Interpretation of the b-Value:
  • b ≈ 1.0 (Equilibrium): Standard tectonic background stress equilibrium (for every one M 6.0 event, there occur ≈ 10 M 5.0 and ≈ 100 M 4.0 events).
  • b < 0.8 (Depressed Stress): High differential shear stress and locked asperities; elevated probability of imminent large-scale seismic rupture.
  • b > 1.2 (Elevated Swarm): Low ambient differential stress or high geothermal fluid pressure, typical of volcanic swarms or hydraulic fracturing.

1.5 Focal Depth Regimes & Intensity Attenuation

Earthquakes are categorized into three depth tiers based on the lithospheric thermal-rheological boundary:

0 – 70 km
Shallow Focus
Comprises over 85% of all global seismic events. Causes maximum destructive surface shaking due to minimal geometric wave attenuation before reaching human infrastructure.
70 – 300 km
Intermediate Focus
Generated within descending oceanic lithospheric slabs (Wadati-Benioff zones). Rarely generates catastrophic surface shaking unless magnitude exceeds Mw 7.5.
300 – 700 km
Deep Focus
Confined to deep subducted slabs where brittle fracture is inhibited by lithostatic confining pressure; driven by mineral phase transitions (e.g. olivine → wadsleyite → ringwoodite).
Magnitude vs. Intensity (MMI & PGA)
Magnitude represents a single, objective measurement of the energy released at the source. Intensity (measured via the Modified Mercalli Intensity scale, MMI I–XII, and Peak Ground Acceleration, PGA) varies continuously based on hypocentral distance, radiation pattern, directivity, and local soil resonance / liquefaction site effects.
SECTION 02 REGIONAL PROFILES CANADA & CHINA

Regional Earthquake Profiles: Canada & China

Comparative tectonic settings, fault systems, active seismic belts, historical benchmark events, and national monitoring authorities.

2.1 Canada Earthquake Profile

Canada experiences approximately 4,000 earthquakes annually, monitored nationally by Natural Resources Canada (NRCan) through the Canadian National Seismograph Network (CNSN). Seismicity is concentrated along the active Pacific Rim margin, the Arctic/Yukon orogeny, and ancient intraplate rift structures in Eastern Canada.

SUBDUCTION
Cascadia Subduction Zone (CSZ)
The Juan de Fuca plate subducts beneath the North American Plate along a 1,000 km megathrust trench from northern Vancouver Island to northern California. Currently fully locked, accumulating strain for the next periodic Mw ≈ 9.0 megathrust earthquake (mean recurrence ~500 years).
TRANSFORM
Queen Charlotte - Fairweather Fault
Canada's counterpart to the San Andreas fault: a high-velocity dextral strike-slip plate boundary separating the Pacific and North American plates off Haida Gwaii, slipping at ≈ 40 mm/year. Host to Canada's largest instrumentally recorded event (1949 Mw 8.1).
INTRAPLATE
St. Lawrence Intraplate Seismic Zone
Ancient Iapetus rift faults reactivated under regional northeast-southwest compressive stress. Includes the active Charlevoix seismic zone (Quebec), where ancestral meteorite impact fractures concentrate persistent intraplate seismicity.
Historical Event Date Magnitude Focal Region Tectonic Setting & Historical Significance
Cascadia Megathrust Jan 26, 1700 M ~ 9.0 Offshore BC / Cascadia Trench Full 1,000 km rupture; triggered an orphan tsunami recorded in coastal chronicles across Japan.
Grand Banks Earthquake Nov 18, 1929 Mw 7.2 Laurentian Slope, Newfoundland Triggered a massive submarine turbidity current severing 12 transatlantic telegraph cables; 28 casualties from tsunami.
Haida Gwaii Strike-Slip Aug 22, 1949 Mw 8.1 Queen Charlotte Fault, BC Canada's largest recorded earthquake; ruptured ~500 km of plate boundary with up to 5 meters of lateral displacement.
Haida Gwaii Thrust Event Oct 27, 2012 Mw 7.7 Moresby Island, BC Oblique thrusting along underthrusting Pacific plate; generated localized 7-meter run-up tsunamis in secluded inlets.

2.2 China Earthquake Profile

China experiences exceptionally high continental seismicity, overseen nationally by the China Earthquake Networks Center (CENC) under the China Earthquake Administration (CEA). Tectonics are dominated by the continuous collision between the Indian Plate and the Eurasian Plate (40 – 50 mm/year convergence), causing rapid uplift and eastward extrusion (continental escape tectonics) of the Tibetan Plateau against rigid cratonic blocks.

COLLISION
Longmenshan Thrust Belt (Sichuan)
Steep topographic and structural boundary between the high Tibetan Plateau and the rigid Sichuan Basin. Compressive thrust and right-lateral strike-slip accumulation resulted in the catastrophic 2008 Wenchuan (Mw 7.9) and 2013 Lushan (Ms 7.0) ruptures.
ESCAPE
Tibetan Escape Faults (Xianshuihe/Kunlun)
Major strike-slip corridors accommodating eastward continental extrusion: left-lateral Altyn Tagh, Kunlun (2001 Ms 8.1), and Xianshuihe faults with slip rates exceeding 10 – 12 mm/year.
CRATONIC
North China Cratonic Graben Systems
Intraplate extension and shear within the ancient Sino-Korean craton. The Shanxi Graben and Bohai Basin systems have historically produced high-fatality shallow events directly beneath dense population centers (1556 Shaanxi, 1976 Tangshan).
Historical Event Date Magnitude Focal Region Tectonic Setting & Historical Significance
Shaanxi (Huaxian) Earthquake Jan 23, 1556 M ~ 8.3 Weihe Graben, Shaanxi Deadliest earthquake in recorded human history (~830,000 fatalities due to catastrophic collapse of loess cave dwellings).
Haiyuan Earthquake Dec 16, 1920 Ms 8.5 Ningxia / Gansu Left-lateral strike-slip rupture along Haiyuan fault (>220 km surface rupture); over 270,000 casualties from massive loess landslides.
Tangshan Earthquake Jul 28, 1976 Ms 7.8 Hebei Province Shallow blind strike-slip fault directly beneath industrial city; ~242,000 casualties; triggered nationwide overhaul of seismic building codes.
Wenchuan (Sichuan) Earthquake May 12, 2008 Mw 7.9 / Ms 8.0 Longmenshan, Sichuan Unilateral 240+ km rupture along Yingxiu-Beichuan fault; >87,000 dead/missing; catalyzed modern real-time automated CENC seismic network.

3.2 Spatial-Temporal Deduplication Algorithm

Because both national networks (NRCan/CENC) and global agencies (USGS) detect and report the same seismic ruptures, ingestion pipelines implement a strict spatial-temporal deduplication algorithm before persistence:

Spatial Proximity Condition (Haversine)
Δd ≤ 25.0 km
Haversine great-circle distance between two candidate epicenter coordinates must be within 25.0 km to account for cross-agency station array variances and regional velocity model differences.
Temporal Coincidence Window
| t₁ - t₂ | ≤ 60.0 seconds
Origin time difference in UTC must fall within ±60.0 seconds. If both spatial and temporal criteria match, candidate records are recognized as the same rupture event.
Deduplication Priority & Attribute Merging
When an event is detected in both a national catalog and USGS, CEMA merges solutions by prioritizing national network precision (NRCan for Canada, CENC for China) for local magnitudes (ML, Ms), while preserving USGS moment tensor solutions (Mw) when available. Events below magnitude M < 3.0 are filtered out to maintain catalog purity and high signal-to-noise ratio.

3.3 Dual Regional SQLite Catalogs & WAL Concurrency

CEMA eliminates shared-state database locking by isolating regional data into three distinct SQLite databases:

CATALOG CAN
data/eq-canada.db
Stores Canadian territorial and maritime seismic events (AOI: 41.7°N → 83.1°N, 141.0°W → 52.6°W). Indexed on origintimeutc, magnitude, latitude, longitude, and province.
CATALOG CHN
data/eq-china.db
Stores Chinese sovereign and continental seismic events (AOI: 18.0°N → 53.6°N, 73.5°E → 135.1°E). Indexed on origintimeutc, magnitude, latitude, longitude, and province.
SECURITY
data/cema-admin.db
Stores administrative passkey hashes, cryptographic audit trails, operator access sessions, and rate-limiting moderation locks. Completely separated from public read queries.
High-Concurrency SQLite Optimization
All database connections are configured with PRAGMA journal_mode=WAL; (Write-Ahead Logging) and PRAGMA synchronous=NORMAL;. This allows concurrent readers on public endpoints to proceed at full memory speed without ever being blocked by background ingestion writes or backfill operations.

3.4 Sovereign Border & Administrative Classification

Unlike raw feeds that often mislabel oceanic or border ruptures with generic geographical strings, CEMA executes a topological ray-casting point-in-polygon algorithm (country_assigner.py) against sovereign national GeoJSON boundaries.

  • Canada: Automatically tagged with 2-letter postal abbreviations (BC, AB, ON, QC, YT, NT, NU, etc.). Events within Canada's 200-nautical-mile Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ) are correctly retained under Canadian jurisdiction.
  • China: Automatically classified by province, autonomous region, or municipality (SC for Sichuan, YN for Yunnan, XJ for Xinjiang, XZ for Tibet, etc.).
  • Cross-Border / International: Events in neighboring territories (e.g. US Alaska, Washington, Myanmar, Nepal) are isolated and categorized by their true sovereign jurisdiction.

3.5 Real-Time Server-Sent Events (SSE) & Synchronized Clocks

Live monitoring is delivered over an asynchronous HTTP Server-Sent Events channel at GET /api/v1/live:

📡 Event Streaming (/api/v1/live)
Maintains a persistent push channel to all connected observatories. When new events pass deduplication, a JSON payload is immediately broadcast to all clients without client polling overhead.
💓 Telemetry Heartbeat (15s)
A periodic keep-alive packet ensures proxy connections and mobile network sockets remain open, broadcasting instantaneous active catalog event counts and server health.
⏱️ Synchronized Dual Clocks
CEMA maintains three synchronized clocks in the header: Canada Mountain Standard Time (MST, UTC-7), China Standard Time (CST, UTC+8), and UTC, providing immediate operational situational awareness.

3.6 Dynamic Acoustic Seismic Sonification

To provide multimodal sensory awareness, CEMA synthesizes acoustic seismic chirps directly inside the browser using the native Web Audio API:

Magnitude-to-Frequency Pitch Mapping
f(M) = 240.0 - (M - 3.0) · 38.0 Hz
Small tremors (M 3.0) produce crisp, higher-frequency clicks (~240 Hz), while major earthquakes (M 7.0+) trigger deep, ominous infrasonic bass rumbles down to 50 – 60 Hz.
Dynamic Gain & Exponential Decay Envelope
Gain(M) = clamp(0.08 + (M - 3.0) · 0.16, 0.08, 0.85)
Rupture acoustic energy uses an exponential decay gain envelope with duration T = 0.25 + (M - 3.0) × 0.20 s, creating a realistic acoustic impulse without needing external audio file downloads.

3.7 Operations Console, Moderation & Data Safeguards

The CEMA Operations Deck (/admin) equips station operators with comprehensive catalog maintenance tools while preventing accidental data destruction:

Explicit Safeguards on Destructive Operations
Any destructive catalog action (e.g. purging date/year ranges or resetting the engine state) requires:
  1. Active administrative passkey authorization against data/cema-admin.db.
  2. Display of a high-contrast danger modal detailing the exact target database (Canada or China), date range, and affected event count.
  3. Explicit, two-step confirmation before executing permanent SQLite DELETE statements.
  4. Real-time logging of the deletion event and operator fingerprint into Operator.Log and admin audit tables.
